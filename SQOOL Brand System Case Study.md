## **The 30-Second Summary**

As UNOWHY rapidly expanded its EdTech presence, the SQOOL ecosystem faced a critical crisis of brand fragmentation, inconsistency, and design debt across its 25+ applications. Serving as Lead Designer, I architected and implemented the comprehensive SQOOL Brand & Design System—a strategic framework encompassing visual identity, component libraries, and editorial guidelines. This initiative became the single source of truth, standardizing the user experience across tablets, web apps, and guides. The result was a dramatic improvement in perceived quality, accelerated product development, and stronger brand recognition across the national education market.

# **The Case Study**

## **01\. The Challenge: Unifying a Fragmented Ecosystem**

The SQOOL platform had grown organically, leading to a sprawling software portfolio where each new product inherited inconsistencies from legacy systems. This lack of centralized design slowed development velocity and created user friction, particularly for teachers moving between unaligned tools (e.g., Classe, Partage, MDM). Our mandate was to impose order, scalability, and brand clarity across the entire product surface.

### **Key Problems Identified:**

* **Visual Debt:** Inconsistent colors, typography, and UI patterns across apps.  
* **Operational Friction:** Slow collaboration due to duplicated components and ambiguous design handoffs.  
* **Brand Confusion:** Diluted identity across physical products, software, and marketing materials.  
* **Editorial Drift:** No centralized tone of voice or naming conventions (UX Writing).

## **02\. Strategy: System Architecture as Brand Foundation**

We approached this not as a simple redesign, but as a holistic system architecture project, prioritizing consistency and scalability. The strategy was divided into three core, interdependent axes: Visual, Editorial, and Product Integration.

### **Phase I: Brand Core Definition**

We collaborated with the Fllow agency to anchor the new SQOOL identity. My role was to translate corporate vision into functional product language.

* **Visual Language:** Defined the core geometric language (e.g., hexagonal shape for app identity) and the use of duotone color systems for product differentiation.  
* **Typographic System:** Selected Inter/Geist for reliability and legibility, codified with tokens for easy maintenance.  
* **Product-Brand Bridge:** Clearly articulated the relationship between the corporate UNOWHY identity and the product-facing SQOOL brand.

### **Phase II: Design System Construction**

The UI Design System served as the operational backbone for development. We built a multi-library Figma kit focused on modularity and cross-platform adaptation (web, tablet).

* **Component Scalability:** Designed components (App Cards, Data Tables, Modals) with clear variants, supporting both desktop and touch-based tablet contexts.  
* **Tokens & Accessibility:** Rigorously defined design tokens (color, spacing, radius) ensuring accessible contrast ratios and font legibility.  
* **Role-Based Color:** Implemented a system where color palettes were tied to user role or app function (e.g., blue for admin/pilotage, green for creation).

## **03\. Execution: Iconography & Editorial Cohesion**

Successful system adoption relies on impeccable details and robust documentation. The execution focused on unifying key elements: app identity and the language used in the interface.

### **Creating a Unified Visual Iconography**

I led the visual explorations for all application, folder, and file icons. The goal was to ensure clarity and recognizability across light and dark themes, even at small sizes. The final system utilized a consistent, minimal duotone structure set within a predefined grid.

* *Initial Exploration:* Tested dozens of styles (shadows, glyphs, metaphors) to assess scalability.  
* *Final System:* Delivered exported icon sets for Android, iOS, and Web platforms.

### **Codifying the Editorial System**

We led workshops to define a global *Tone of Voice* (reassuring, simple, pragmatic) and a strict terminology map, ensuring consistency across error messages, button labels, and user guides.

* **Example Rule:** Simplified button labels for better context and fit on small screens (e.g., "Start a Virtual PC Session" became "Launch a PC Session").

### **Documentation & Governance**

The entire system was documented on a Zeroheight site, serving as the single source of truth for all 5+ product squads, marketing, and support teams. This was the key to cultural adoption.

## **04\. Impact & Outcomes: Scaling with Confidence**

The SQOOL Brand & Design System became foundational, transforming how products were scoped, designed, and built across the organization.

### **Tangible Results:**

* **100% Visual Cohesion:** The entire SQOOL Apps Suite (25+ products) achieved a high level of visual and functional consistency, drastically improving the perceived quality of the platform.  
* **Accelerated Velocity:** Designers reported a **30% reduction in design time**, allowing them to focus on complex problem-solving rather than foundational UI work.  
* **Enhanced Brand Recognition:** The unified look and feel strengthened brand presence across hardware, OS, and web services, making it easier for institutions to recognize and trust the platform.  
* **Scalability Confirmed:** The system was successfully adopted by all product teams and served as the default basis for new product launches (e.g., SQOOL Protect, Extend).

## **Key Learnings**

1. **System Adoption is Cultural:** The success of the design system was not in the Figma file, but in the rituals—weekly syncs, design QA, and robust documentation—that drove cross-functional adoption.  
2. **Documentation Before Code:** Rigorous, upfront documentation (Zeroheight guide) of usage guidelines and edge cases was essential to ensure long-term component health and trust from engineering teams.  
3. **Visual Language Drives Strategy:** The process of defining the visual and editorial language helped align product teams on shared principles, turning the design system into a critical product strategy tool.
